How many sets do you need?

A simple way to estimate is:

  1. Count the patients per chair in your busiest session for each procedure type.
  2. Estimate how long a set takes to clean, sterilize and dry, including waiting time.
  3. Calculate how many sets are in reprocessing at any time and add these to the sets in use.
  4. Add a small reserve for damage and unexpected demand.

Examination sets are usually needed in the largest numbers, because almost every appointment uses one.

Buying tips

Buying checklist

  • Standardise patterns across the clinic so sets are interchangeable.
  • Choose handle styles your clinicians find comfortable.
  • Invest in quality for high-use instruments such as scalers and forceps.
  • Plan for cassettes and storage from the start.

Frequently asked questions

Should a new clinic buy complete kits or individual instruments?

Kits are convenient, but buying individual instruments to your own list avoids paying for items you will not use.

Which extraction forceps are essential?

Most clinics start with upper and lower universals, upper and lower molar forceps and root forceps, then add others.

How often will instruments need replacing?

It depends on use and care. Scalers and curettes wear fastest because of sharpening.
