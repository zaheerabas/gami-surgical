Hinged instruments such as scissors, forceps, needle holders and hemostats have metal surfaces that rub against each other every time they open and close. Without lubrication, joints become stiff, wear faster and can start to corrode. Instrument lubrication is a quick step that adds years to an instrument's life.

In short

Lubricate hinged instruments after cleaning and before sterilization with a water-soluble, steam-permeable instrument lubricant. Do not use mineral oil, silicone sprays or household oils.

Why instrument lubrication matters

Cleaning removes not only debris but also any lubricant in the joints. Metal-to-metal contact then causes friction, stiffness and galling (surface damage from rubbing). Stiff instruments are harder to use and wear their joints and ratchets faster.

Which lubricant?

LubricantSuitable?Why
Water-soluble instrument lubricant ("instrument milk")YesSteam can penetrate it; compatible with sterilization
Mineral oilNoBlocks steam and traps residue
Silicone spraysNoLeaves a film that can interfere with sterilization
Household or machine oilsNoNot designed for medical use

Always check that the lubricant is compatible with your sterilization method and follow its dilution instructions.

When and how to lubricate

  1. Clean and rinse instruments thoroughly.
  2. Apply lubricant, for example by dipping in a diluted bath or spraying, with instruments open.
  3. Work the joints open and closed to spread the lubricant.
  4. Allow excess to drain; do not wipe it off unless the instructions say so.
  5. Package and sterilize.

Common mistakes

  • Lubricating before cleaning, which traps debris.
  • Using the wrong lubricant.
  • Over-diluting or reusing old lubricant baths.
  • Skipping lubrication for instruments that "still move".

Frequently asked questions

How often should instruments be lubricated?

Many facilities lubricate hinged instruments after every cleaning cycle, before sterilization.

Do washer-disinfectors lubricate instruments?

Some have a lubrication stage. Check whether yours does and that it uses a suitable lubricant.

Will lubrication fix a stiff instrument?

Often, if the stiffness is due to dryness. If a joint stays stiff, it may need cleaning or adjustment by a repair service.
