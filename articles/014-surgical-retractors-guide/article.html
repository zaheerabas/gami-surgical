Surgical retractors hold tissue apart so the surgeon can see and work. Some are held by an assistant; others hold themselves open with a ratchet or screw. Choosing the right retractor depends on how deep the wound is, how much force is needed and how delicate the tissue is.

In short

Hand-held retractors are flexible and quick to reposition; self-retaining retractors free up an assistant. Rakes and hooks suit skin and superficial tissue, smooth blades suit deeper layers. Sharp prongs grip well but can injure tissue; blunt prongs are gentler.

Skin hooks, single or double, are the gentlest option for lifting skin edges in fine and plastic surgery.

Self-retaining retractors

Self-retaining retractors stay open on their own, which is useful when an assistant is not available or when retraction must be held steady for a long time.

  • Weitlaner: a ratcheted retractor with rake-like prongs on both arms, sharp or blunt, used in many superficial and orthopaedic procedures.
  • Gelpi: two arms ending in single sharp points, useful in deep, narrow incisions.
  • West and similar patterns: smaller ratcheted retractors for shallow wounds.
  • Frame retractors such as the Balfour, used in abdominal surgery, with several interchangeable blades.

Sharp or blunt prongs?

Sharp prongs grip firmly and are less likely to slip, but they can puncture vessels, nerves or gloves. Blunt prongs are safer around delicate structures. Many departments keep both and choose according to the tissue.

What to specify when ordering

Ordering checklist

  • Pattern name and overall length.
  • Blade size, depth and width, or the number of prongs on a rake.
  • Sharp or blunt prongs.
  • For self-retaining retractors: the spread when fully open and the type of lock.
  • Single instruments or pairs (hand-held retractors are often used in pairs).

Care and inspection

Check prongs for bends and burrs after each use, because a bent prong can tear tissue. On self-retaining retractors, make sure the ratchet engages and releases cleanly. Clean them in the open position so the ratchet and joint can be reached.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between a Senn and an Army-Navy retractor?

Both are double-ended hand-held retractors. The Senn has a small rake on one end and a narrow blade on the other, for small, superficial wounds. The Army-Navy has two L-shaped blades of different depths for slightly deeper wounds.

When should I use a self-retaining retractor?

Use one when retraction has to be held steady for a long time or when no assistant is free to hold a hand-held retractor.

Are sharp rakes safe?

Sharp rakes grip well but can puncture vessels, nerves and gloves. Use blunt rakes near delicate structures.
