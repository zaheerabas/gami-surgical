Tonsillectomy is one of the most common ENT operations, and most hospitals keep a dedicated tonsil set. Tonsillectomy instruments are designed to hold the mouth open, grasp the tonsil, dissect it free and control bleeding in a narrow working space.

Note

Techniques and preferred instruments vary between surgeons. This overview helps buyers understand typical contents; confirm the final set with your ENT team.

Mouth gags

The mouth gag is the centre of the tonsil set. Boyle-Davis gags are widely used, with interchangeable tongue blades in several sizes for children and adults. Make sure the blades and gag frame are compatible when ordering.

Paediatric and adult sizes

Many tonsillectomies are performed on children, so sets often include smaller tongue blades and instruments. Some hospitals keep separate paediatric and adult sets.

Buying checklist

  • Confirm the mouth gag pattern and the range of tongue blade sizes.
  • Check compatibility between gags, blades and suspension.
  • Include both artery forceps and a ligature method your surgeons use.
  • Choose long instruments with good balance for work at depth.

Frequently asked questions

What is a Boyle-Davis mouth gag?

A widely used mouth gag with interchangeable tongue blades, designed to hold the mouth open during tonsil surgery.

Are tonsillectomy instruments different for children?

The instrument types are the same, but smaller tongue blades and sometimes smaller instruments are used.

Do modern techniques still need these instruments?

Even when energy devices are used, most surgeons still rely on a mouth gag, holding forceps and artery forceps.
