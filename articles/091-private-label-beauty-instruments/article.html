Many beauty brands, salon chains and distributors sell tweezers, nippers and scissors under their own name. Private label beauty instruments are made by a manufacturer and finished, marked and packed for your brand. This guide walks through the steps of launching a range.

In short

Start with a small, focused range, approve samples carefully, choose durable marking and finishes, and plan packaging and quantities with your manufacturer.

Step 1: Choose your range

Start with best-selling tools, such as slant tweezers, cuticle nippers, cuticle pushers and a manicure set. A focused range is easier to launch and stock. Add products as you learn what your customers want.

Step 2: Specify each product

DetailExamples
Design and sizeTip shape, jaw size, length

BrandingLaser-marked logo, position, size
PackagingPouch, blister card, case, gift box

Step 3: Approve samples

Ask for samples of each item with your logo and finish. Test them: tip alignment, cutting quality, how coatings survive cleaning, and how the packaging protects the tool. Keep approved samples as the reference for production.

Step 4: Plan quantities and reorders

Private label production often has minimum quantities per design, colour or packaging style. Discuss these early, along with production lead times, so you can plan launches and reorders.

Step 5: Labelling and compliance

Check the labelling rules in the countries where you will sell, such as material, origin and safety information, and make sure packaging artwork includes them.

Launch checklist

  • A clear product list with specifications.
  • Approved samples for every product.
  • Logo artwork in a format suitable for laser marking.
  • Packaging artwork with barcodes and labels.
  • Agreed quantities, lead times and shipping method.

Frequently asked questions

How is a logo applied to beauty tools?

Laser marking is common because it is permanent and precise. Printing and etching are other options.

Can I choose my own colours?

Usually yes, within the coatings the manufacturer offers. Ask for colour samples.

Is private label only for large brands?

No. Salons and small brands also launch ranges; discuss realistic starting quantities with your manufacturer.
