Self-retaining retractors hold a wound open by themselves, using a ratchet, screw or frame. They give a steady, hands-free view and free an assistant for other tasks. This guide compares the most common self-retaining retractors and the details that matter when choosing them.

In short

Weitlaner retractors have rakes and suit superficial wounds; Gelpi retractors have single points and suit deep, narrow wounds. Choose the size and depth for the incision, and sharp or blunt prongs for the tissue.

Weitlaner retractor

The Weitlaner has two arms, each ending in a rake of several prongs (often described as 3x4 or 2x3), and a ratchet near the finger rings. It is used widely in general, orthopaedic and spinal surgery for superficial to medium-depth incisions. It comes in several lengths and with sharp or blunt prongs.

Gelpi retractor

The Gelpi has two curved arms that each end in a single, outward-turned point. Because it holds tissue at one point on each side, it suits deep, narrow incisions and works well in orthopaedic and spinal procedures. The points are usually sharp, so care is needed near delicate structures.

Other patterns

  • West: a small ratcheted retractor with rake prongs, often with a slight angle, for shallow wounds.
  • Adson-Beckman and Beckman patterns: larger ratcheted retractors, sometimes with hinged arms, for deeper work.
  • Frame retractors (for example Balfour for abdominal surgery): a frame with interchangeable blades that hold a large incision open.

Choosing the right size

Self-retaining retractors are ordered by overall length, and the length roughly reflects the depth and spread they can achieve. Too small and they cannot open the wound enough; too large and they get in the way. Many departments stock two or three sizes of the same pattern.

What to check before buying

Quality checklist

  • The ratchet engages firmly on every tooth and releases smoothly.
  • The arms open evenly and the prongs meet symmetrically when closed.
  • Prongs are straight and evenly finished, with no burrs.
  • The joint has no side-to-side play.

Care

Clean self-retaining retractors in the open position, brushing the ratchet and the joint, and lubricate them with a water-soluble lubricant. Inspect prongs for bending after every use; a bent or damaged prong should be repaired or the instrument replaced.

Frequently asked questions

Is a Weitlaner or a Gelpi better for deep wounds?

The Gelpi usually works better in deep, narrow wounds because its single points take up little space. The Weitlaner's rakes spread the load over more tissue and suit shallower, wider incisions.

What does 3x4 mean on a Weitlaner retractor?

It describes the number of prongs on each arm: three on one side and four on the other, so the rakes interlock when closed.

Can a bent prong be repaired?

Often yes. An instrument repair service can straighten and refinish prongs, but a cracked prong should be replaced.
