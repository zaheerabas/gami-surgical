When a root tip fractures during an extraction, it can be difficult to remove with forceps or standard elevators. Root tip picks are very fine instruments with slender, sharp tips that slip between the root fragment and the socket wall to tease it out.

In short

Root tip picks have very fine, angled tips for small root fragments. They come straight and in left and right versions. Their tips are delicate and must not be used for heavy leverage.

Design

Root tip picks have a long, slim shank that ends in a small, sharp, angled tip. The fine tip can enter the narrow space around a root apex, where a standard elevator would not fit. Light pressure is used to loosen and lift the fragment.

Protecting the tips

Because the working ends are so fine, root tip picks are easily bent or broken if used with too much force. Using them only for gentle teasing, and switching to an elevator when more force is needed, keeps them in shape.

Care checklist

  • Inspect tips under magnification after each use.
  • Keep them in cassettes with tip protection.
  • Do not use them as levers against bone or teeth.
  • Replace picks with bent or blunt tips.

Frequently asked questions

When are root tip picks used?

When a small fragment of root remains in the socket and needs to be loosened and lifted out.

Why do they come in left and right versions?

Angled tips are mirror images so the clinician can approach the root from either side.

Can root tip picks be sharpened?

Fine tips can sometimes be reshaped by a specialist, but they are usually replaced once worn.
