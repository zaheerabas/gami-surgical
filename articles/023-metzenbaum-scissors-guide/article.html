Metzenbaum scissors, often shortened to "Metz", are the standard scissors for cutting and dissecting delicate tissue. Their long handles and relatively short blades give reach and fine control, which is why they appear in almost every general surgery set. They are also among the instruments most often damaged by misuse.

In short

Use Metzenbaum scissors for delicate tissue and blunt dissection only. Choose curved blades for dissection, the length for the depth of the wound, and consider supercut or tungsten carbide edges for longer life.

What Metzenbaum scissors are for

The long handle-to-blade ratio lets the surgeon work deep in a wound while keeping the hand clear of the field. The fine blades cut delicate tissue cleanly, and the closed tips are used for blunt dissection: pushed gently into a tissue plane and opened to separate layers.

They are not meant for sutures, drapes or dressings. Cutting these dulls the fine edges quickly, and blunt Metzenbaum scissors crush tissue instead of cutting it.

Curved or straight

Curved Metzenbaum scissors are the more common choice because the curve lets the user see the tips and follow tissue planes. Straight versions are used for cutting in more superficial or open areas.

Tips and edges

  • Blunt/blunt tips are standard and are safer for dissection near vessels and organs.
  • Supercut versions have one razor-sharp blade for an extra-smooth cut; they are often recognised by black handles.
  • Tungsten carbide (TC) versions, with gold handles, keep their edge much longer.
  • Delicate or "slim" patterns have narrower blades for very fine work.

Keeping Metzenbaum scissors sharp

Care checklist

  • Keep them for tissue only; give the team suture scissors for sutures.
  • Protect the tips with tip guards during reprocessing.
  • Clean them open and lubricate the joint after cleaning.
  • Test them regularly and send them for sharpening when they start to chew or fold tissue.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between Metzenbaum and Mayo scissors?

Metzenbaum scissors have long handles and slim blades for delicate tissue. Mayo scissors are heavier, with shorter handles and broader blades, for tougher tissue such as fascia, and straight Mayo scissors are used for sutures.

Can Metzenbaum scissors be sharpened?

Yes, standard and tungsten carbide versions can be resharpened by a professional repair service. Supercut blades need a specialist who can restore the razor edge.

Which size should I order first?

If you are unsure, an 18 cm curved pair is the most common general surgery size.
