Every international shipment of instruments travels with a set of documents. Customs authorities use them to classify the goods, calculate duties and taxes, and check that import rules are met. Missing or inconsistent customs documents are one of the most common reasons for delays.

In short

Expect a commercial invoice, a packing list and a transport document (air waybill, courier waybill or bill of lading). Depending on your country and products, you may also need a certificate of origin and regulatory documents.

The main customs documents

DocumentPurpose
Commercial invoiceDescribes the goods, quantities, values, currency, Incoterm, seller and buyer
Packing listLists the contents of each carton, with weights and dimensions
Air waybill / courier waybill / bill of ladingTransport contract and receipt for the goods
Certificate of originProves where the goods were made; can reduce duties under trade agreements
Regulatory documentsRegistrations, declarations or certificates required for medical devices in some markets

HS codes

Customs classify goods using Harmonized System (HS) codes. Codes commonly used for instruments include 9018.90 for many surgical instruments, 9018.49 for dental instruments, 8214.20 for manicure and pedicure instruments and 8203.20 for tweezers. National tariffs add further digits. Confirm the correct code with your customs broker, as classification affects duty rates.

Avoiding delays

Document checklist

  • Descriptions on the invoice are specific (for example "stainless steel surgical scissors"), not vague.
  • Quantities and values match across invoice and packing list.
  • The Incoterm is stated and matches the contract.
  • The importer's details and any tax or import numbers are correct.
  • Regulatory requirements for medical devices in your country are checked in advance.

Medical device import rules

In many countries, surgical and dental instruments are regulated as medical devices. Importers may need to be registered, and products may need to be listed or meet labelling requirements before they can be imported or sold. Check with your regulator or a regulatory consultant before your first order.

Frequently asked questions

Who prepares the customs documents?

The seller usually prepares the invoice, packing list and certificate of origin; the carrier issues the transport document; the importer or broker handles the import declaration.

Do samples need customs documents?

Yes. Even samples sent by courier need an invoice showing their value.

Can a certificate of origin reduce duties?

Under some trade agreements and preference schemes it can. Ask your broker.
