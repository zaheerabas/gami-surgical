Bone rongeurs are heavy, spring-handled instruments with sharp, cup-shaped jaws that nibble away bone. They are used in orthopaedic, spinal, neurosurgical, ENT and dental surgery to trim bone edges, remove fragments and shape bone. Choosing the right bone rongeurs depends on how much force is needed and how precise the bite must be.

In short

Single-action rongeurs are simple and suit lighter work. Double-action rongeurs use a compound joint to multiply hand force for dense bone. Kerrison rongeurs punch small pieces of bone in narrow spaces such as the spine.

Single-action and double-action

In a single-action rongeur, the handles connect directly to the jaws at one pivot. In a double-action (compound) rongeur, two joints work together to increase the cutting force, so the surgeon can bite through dense bone with less effort and more control.

Kerrison rongeurs

Kerrison rongeurs have a fixed footplate and a sliding upper blade. The footplate slips under the bone edge and the blade punches out a small piece. They are made with different bite sizes (often 1 to 6 mm) and angles (for example 40 or 90 degrees, up-biting or down-biting), so all three details should be specified when ordering.

What to check

Quality checklist

  • The jaws close evenly and the cutting edges meet along their full length.
  • The cups are sharp and free of nicks.
  • Springs return the handles smoothly and are firmly attached.
  • On Kerrison rongeurs, the slide moves smoothly without play.

Care and cleaning

Bone fragments and tissue collect in the cups and joints. Clean bone rongeurs promptly, open the jaws fully, and brush the cups and joints. Many Kerrison rongeurs can be disassembled for cleaning; follow the manufacturer's instructions. Have the cutting edges sharpened regularly.

Frequently asked questions

What does double-action mean on a rongeur?

It means the instrument has a compound joint that increases the force at the jaws, making it easier to cut dense bone.

How are Kerrison rongeurs sized?

By bite width in millimetres, angle (for example 40 or 90 degrees), direction (up or down) and shaft length.

Can rongeurs be sharpened?

Yes. A specialist repair service can sharpen the cups and adjust the jaws.
