Dermal curettes are scraping instruments with a small, sharp-edged cup or loop at the tip. They are used in dermatology and minor surgery to remove soft skin lesions and to clean wounds and cavities. Unlike a scalpel, a curette removes soft tissue while largely sparing the firmer tissue beneath.

In short

Choose the cup size to match the lesion, keep the edge sharp, and use round or oval cups depending on the area. Reusable stainless steel curettes need regular inspection and sharpening.

How a curette works

The cup has a sharp rim on one side. When the curette is drawn across the skin, the rim scoops out soft, abnormal tissue, which tends to separate more easily than healthy tissue. This difference is why curettage is used for certain superficial lesions, often combined with other treatment.

Other surgical curettes

Curettes are also used in many other specialties, with different designs: bone curettes in orthopaedics, ear curettes in ENT and uterine curettes in gynaecology. They are larger or shaped differently, so specify the specialty and pattern when ordering.

Keeping dermal curettes sharp

Care checklist

  • Inspect the rim under magnification for nicks and dull areas.
  • Clean the cup carefully; tissue can collect inside it.
  • Store curettes so the rims do not touch other instruments.
  • Have them sharpened when they start to slide instead of scoop.

Frequently asked questions

What size dermal curette should I buy first?

Many clinics start with a mid-range size, such as 3 to 4 mm, and add smaller and larger sizes as needed.

Are reusable curettes better than disposable ones?

Reusable stainless steel curettes can be resharpened and are cheaper per use over time. Disposable curettes are always sharp but add waste and cost per procedure.

Can a curette replace a scalpel?

No. They do different jobs: a scalpel cuts precisely, while a curette scrapes away soft tissue.
