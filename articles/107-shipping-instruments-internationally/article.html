For buyers ordering from a manufacturer abroad, the shipping method affects cost, speed and how much paperwork is involved. Shipping instruments internationally is usually done by express courier, air freight or sea freight. This guide compares them and explains how instruments should be packed.

In short

Use express courier for samples and small or urgent orders, air freight for medium orders needed quickly, and sea freight for large orders where cost matters more than time.

Shipping instruments internationally: methods compared

Actual transit times depend on the route, season and customs clearance.

Packing for international transport

Good packing

  • Instruments dry and lightly protected against moisture.
  • Individual pouches or wraps so instruments do not rub together.
  • Tip protectors on sharp and fine instruments.
  • Strong outer cartons, filled to prevent movement.
  • Clear carton labels and a packing list matching the contents.

Insurance and tracking

Couriers provide tracking and offer insurance options. For air and sea freight, the forwarder provides tracking and insurance can be arranged. Who arranges and pays for insurance depends on the agreed Incoterm.

Customs clearance

Couriers usually handle customs clearance for you and add duties and taxes to their invoice or collect them on delivery. For air and sea freight, a customs broker normally clears the goods. In every case, accurate invoices and packing lists speed things up.

Frequently asked questions

Which shipping method is cheapest?

Sea freight is usually cheapest per item for large orders, but it is slow and has fixed costs that make it uneconomic for small shipments.

Can I combine shipping methods?

Yes. Some buyers send an urgent part of an order by courier and the rest by sea.

Who pays the import duties?

It depends on the Incoterm agreed. Under DDP the seller pays; under most other terms, the buyer pays.
