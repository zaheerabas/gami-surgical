Osteotomes, chisels and gouges are bone-cutting instruments driven by a mallet. They look similar, but the shape of the blade and its bevel decide how each one cuts. Using the right one makes bone work more precise and predictable.

In short

Osteotomes are bevelled on both sides and cut straight through bone. Chisels are bevelled on one side and shave or shape bone. Gouges have a curved blade that scoops bone. Mallets come in different weights to control force.

Osteotomes

An osteotome has a blade that tapers equally from both sides to the cutting edge (a double bevel). When struck, it travels straight into bone and is used to cut or divide it. Lambotte osteotomes are among the best-known patterns and come in a range of widths. Curved osteotomes are also available.

Chisels

A chisel has a single bevel: one side is flat and the other is angled. Because of this, it tends to move away from the flat side and is used to shave, sculpt or remove thin layers of bone.

Gouges

A gouge has a curved, trough-shaped blade that scoops bone, for example to harvest bone graft or shape a cavity.

Widths and sizes

These instruments are ordered by blade width, commonly from a few millimetres up to 25 mm or more, and by overall length. Sets often include several widths so the surgeon can choose the right one for the bone.

Mallets

Surgical mallets are made in different weights and head materials. Heavier mallets deliver more force per blow; lighter mallets give finer control. Some mallets have replaceable nylon or polymer faces to reduce rebound and noise. Choose the weight to suit the procedures and the instruments being driven.

Care

Care checklist

  • Inspect cutting edges for chips and dull areas after every use.
  • Protect edges with guards in trays.
  • Check that handles and striking caps are secure.
  • Send osteotomes and chisels for sharpening regularly.

Frequently asked questions

Can a chisel be used instead of an osteotome?

It can, but a chisel tends to drift because of its single bevel. An osteotome cuts straighter through bone.

What width osteotome should a basic set include?

Many sets include a range, for example narrow, medium and wide blades, so the surgeon can match the blade to the bone.

Are osteotomes sharpened like knives?

They need specialist sharpening that keeps the original bevel angles. Ask a surgical instrument repair service.
