Veterinary surgical instruments are largely the same patterns as those used in human surgery, chosen and sized for animal patients. Small-animal practices use instruments very similar to human general surgery sets, while large-animal and farm practices add heavier, specialised tools.

In short

Most veterinary surgical instruments are standard surgical patterns. Popular choices in small-animal practice include Olsen-Hegar needle holders, spay hooks and Rochester-Carmalt forceps. Large-animal practice adds heavier specialised tools.

Why the Olsen-Hegar is so popular with vets

Vets often operate without an assistant. The Olsen-Hegar needle holder combines a needle holder and scissors, so the surgeon can tie and cut sutures without changing instruments.

Large-animal instruments

Farm and equine practices use heavier instruments, such as hoof knives and hoof testers, emasculators, obstetric instruments and larger needle holders and scissors. These must be robust enough for large patients and field conditions.

Buying checklist

  • Choose sizes suited to your typical patients.
  • Build standard packs (spay, castration, general) for consistency.
  • Choose durable stainless steel that withstands frequent autoclaving.
  • Consider tungsten carbide needle holders and scissors for high-use packs.

Frequently asked questions

Are veterinary surgical instruments different from human ones?

For small animals they are largely the same patterns. Large-animal practice uses some specialised, heavier tools.

What is a spay hook used for?

A spay hook (for example Snook or Covault) is used during ovariohysterectomy to locate and lift the uterine horn through a small incision.

Which needle holder do vets prefer?

Many prefer the Olsen-Hegar because it combines a needle holder and scissors.
