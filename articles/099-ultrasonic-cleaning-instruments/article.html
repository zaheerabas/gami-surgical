Ultrasonic cleaning uses high-frequency sound waves in a liquid to remove debris from places brushes cannot reach, such as box locks, serrations and ratchets. It is widely used in sterile services departments, dental clinics and other settings as part of instrument reprocessing.

In short

Remove gross soil first, open instruments, don't overload the basket, use the right solution at the right temperature, degas fresh solution, change it regularly and test the cleaner's performance.

How ultrasonic cleaning works

The ultrasonic cleaner creates millions of tiny bubbles in the cleaning solution that form and collapse rapidly. This process, called cavitation, produces scrubbing action at the surface of instruments, including inside joints and crevices.

Step by step

  1. Pre-clean: remove visible soil by rinsing or brushing.
  2. Fill the tank with a detergent recommended for ultrasonic use, at the correct dilution and temperature.
  3. Degas fresh solution by running the cleaner empty for the time the manufacturer recommends.
  4. Load instruments open, in a basket, without overloading or stacking.
  5. Run the cycle for the recommended time with the lid closed.
  6. Rinse thoroughly, then dry and inspect.

Instruments that should not go in

Some items can be damaged by ultrasonic cleaning, for example certain optics, powered instruments, and some items with cemented or plated parts. Always check the instrument manufacturer's instructions.

Testing performance

Ultrasonic cleaners lose performance over time. Simple tests, such as foil tests or commercial test indicators, show whether cavitation is reaching the whole tank. Many facilities test daily or weekly as part of their quality system.

Frequently asked questions

Does ultrasonic cleaning replace manual cleaning?

No. It is usually one step in the process, after removing gross soil and before rinsing and drying.

Why degas the solution?

Fresh liquid contains dissolved air that weakens cavitation. Degassing removes it so cleaning is effective.

How often should the solution be changed?

Whenever it is visibly soiled and according to the detergent manufacturer's guidance, often at least daily in busy settings.
