Ear instruments are small and delicate because the ear canal is narrow and sensitive. ENT clinics, audiology services and general practices use a small set of them for examination, wax removal and foreign body removal. Good quality instruments make these procedures quicker and more comfortable for the patient.

In short

A basic ear set includes aural specula in several sizes, Hartmann (alligator) ear forceps, ear curettes or a Jobson Horne probe, and an ear hook. All should have smooth, fine tips.

Hartmann ear forceps

Hartmann forceps, often called alligator forceps, have a long slim shaft and tiny jaws that open with a scissor-style handle. Only the jaws move, so the shaft stays still in the ear canal. They are made with smooth or serrated jaws and in different lengths.

Aural specula

Ear specula are funnel-shaped and come in sets of diameters. Larger sizes give a wider view in adults; smaller sizes suit children and narrow canals. Reusable stainless steel specula can be sterilized repeatedly.

Quality checklist

  • Tips are fine, smooth and rounded, with no sharp edges.
  • Forceps jaws meet precisely and open smoothly.
  • Curette loops are even and correctly angled.
  • Specula have smooth rims and a polished inner surface.

Frequently asked questions

What are alligator forceps?

Another name for Hartmann ear forceps: fine forceps with small jaws at the end of a long shaft, opened by a scissor-style handle.

How many sizes of ear specula are needed?

Most clinics use a set of three to five diameters to suit adults and children.

Can ear instruments be autoclaved?

Stainless steel ear instruments can be cleaned and steam sterilized. Fine tips should be protected during reprocessing.
