Dental tweezers, often called cotton pliers or College pliers, are angled tweezers used to place and remove cotton rolls, pellets, wedges and small items in the mouth. They are part of the basic examination kit and are used in almost every appointment.

In short

Dental tweezers are angled for access in the mouth. Serrated tips grip cotton securely; smooth tips handle delicate items. Locking versions hold items without hand pressure.

Why the angle?

The angle at the tip lets the clinician reach behind teeth and into the back of the mouth while keeping the hand outside the mouth and out of the line of sight.

What makes good dental tweezers

Quality checklist

  • Tips meet exactly along their gripping surfaces.
  • Serrations mesh evenly and grip cotton without tearing it.
  • Spring tension is light and even.
  • Locks engage and release smoothly on locking versions.

Care

Dental tweezers are easily bent if dropped or used to pry. Store them in cassettes so the tips stay aligned, and replace pairs whose tips no longer meet.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between cotton pliers and College pliers?

The terms are often used for the same instrument: angled dental tweezers with serrated tips.

When are locking pliers useful?

When an item must be held for some time, such as a gutta-percha point, without the clinician squeezing the instrument.

Can dental tweezers be autoclaved?

Stainless steel dental tweezers can be cleaned and steam sterilized with other dental instruments.
