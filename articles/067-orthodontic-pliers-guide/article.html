Orthodontists use a range of specialised pliers to bend wires, place and remove bands and brackets, and cut wires and ligatures. Orthodontic pliers are precision tools: jaws must meet accurately, joints must stay tight, and cutters must cut cleanly through hard wire without damage.

In short

Orthodontic pliers fall into three groups: wire-bending pliers, cutters, and pliers for placing or removing bands and brackets. Tungsten carbide inserts on cutting edges and working tips help them last.

Cutters and hard wire

Cutters must be rated for the wire they cut. Using a fine ligature cutter on heavy stainless steel wire can chip the edges. Many cutters have tungsten carbide inserts and state a maximum wire size; check this when ordering.

Joints: box joint or lap joint

Box joints are strong and keep the jaws aligned, which suits pliers that see heavy use. Some pliers use other joint designs. Whatever the design, the joint should be tight with no side play.

Quality checklist

  • Jaws and beaks meet accurately when closed.
  • Cutters cut cleanly at the tips and hold the cut end if designed to.
  • Joints are smooth and tight.
  • Working surfaces are polished so they do not mark wires.

Frequently asked questions

What is a distal end cutter?

A cutter that trims the end of an archwire at the back of the mouth and holds the cut piece so it does not fall into the mouth.

Why do orthodontic pliers have tungsten carbide inserts?

Carbide is very hard, so cutting edges and wire-bending tips stay sharp and precise for longer.

How should orthodontic pliers be maintained?

Clean and dry them fully after sterilization and lubricate the joints regularly to prevent stiffness and corrosion.
