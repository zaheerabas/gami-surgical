Straight razors give the closest shave and the sharpest lines for barbers. Today, most barbers use shavettes, straight razors that hold a replaceable blade, because a fresh blade can be used for every client. Traditional straight razors with a fixed blade are still popular for personal use and with collectors.

In short

Shavettes use replaceable blades, which supports hygiene in barbershops. Traditional straight razors need honing and stropping. Choose a razor that feels balanced and holds the blade securely.

Traditional straight razors

A traditional straight razor has a fixed steel blade that folds into the handle (the scales). The edge is maintained by stropping on leather and periodic honing on a stone. With care, a good straight razor can last a lifetime.

Shavettes (replaceable-blade razors)

A shavette holds a disposable blade, often a half of a double-edge blade or a single-edge blade. The barber replaces the blade for each client, which is why shavettes are widely used in barbershops and are required in some places.

What to look for in a shavette

Quality checklist

  • The blade holder grips the blade firmly with no movement.
  • The pivot is tight but smooth.
  • The handle is balanced and comfortable.
  • Stainless steel construction that can be cleaned and disinfected.
  • Blade loading is quick and safe.

Safety and hygiene

Dispose of used blades in a sharps container, never in general waste. Clean and disinfect the razor between clients according to local rules, and close or cover the blade when not in use.

Frequently asked questions

Why do barbers use shavettes?

Because a new blade can be used for each client, which supports hygiene, and there is no need to hone or strop.

Are straight razors sharper than shavettes?

A well-honed straight razor and a fresh shavette blade are both extremely sharp. The feel of the shave differs slightly.

Can straight razors be branded?

Shavettes and razors can often be finished and marked for barber brands; ask your manufacturer.
