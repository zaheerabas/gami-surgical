Dental restorative instruments are used to place, pack, shape and finish filling materials. Most clinics keep a restorative cassette containing a small set of these hand instruments. Good instruments are well balanced, have smooth working ends and do not stick to modern composite materials.

In short

A restorative set usually includes plastic filling instruments, condensers (pluggers), burnishers, carvers and, where amalgam is still used, an amalgam carrier. Coated or highly polished tips help prevent composite sticking.

The main restorative instruments

InstrumentPurpose
Plastic filling instrumentPlacing and shaping filling material (the name refers to the material, not the instrument)
Composite instrumentsThin, often coated blades and spatulas for sculpting composite
Condensers (pluggers)Packing material into the cavity
BurnishersSmoothing and adapting margins (ball, egg or acorn shapes)
CarversCarving anatomy, for example Hollenback, Frahm or discoid-cleoid
Amalgam carrierCarrying amalgam to the cavity
Calcium hydroxide / lining applicatorPlacing liners with a small ball end

Composite-friendly tips

Composite resins can stick to instruments and pull away from the cavity. Instruments with titanium nitride or other non-stick coatings, or very highly polished tips, help the material release cleanly. Many clinicians keep a separate composite set for this reason.

Double-ended instruments

Most restorative instruments are double-ended, combining two related shapes or sizes, which keeps cassettes compact. Check which combination each pattern offers before ordering.

Quality checklist

  • Working ends are smooth, polished and correctly shaped.
  • Coatings are even and durable.
  • Handles are light and balanced, with a secure grip.
  • Pattern names or numbers are marked on the handle.

Frequently asked questions

Why is it called a plastic filling instrument?

"Plastic" refers to the soft, mouldable state of the filling material, not to what the instrument is made of. Most are stainless steel.

Do I need separate instruments for composite?

It helps. Coated or polished composite instruments reduce sticking and give a smoother finish.

How should coated instruments be cleaned?

Use soft brushes and avoid abrasive cleaners, which can wear the coating.
