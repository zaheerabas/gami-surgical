Many instruments are available both as single-use (disposable) items and as reusable stainless steel instruments. The choice affects cost, performance, waste and workload. There is no single right answer; comparing single-use vs reusable instruments carefully helps each facility choose what suits it.

In short

Reusable instruments usually cost less per use and perform better, but need reliable cleaning and sterilization. Single-use instruments avoid reprocessing but add cost per procedure and waste, and quality varies.

When reusable instruments make sense

  • High-volume procedures, where cost per use matters.
  • Facilities with reliable sterile services.
  • Procedures where instrument performance is critical.
  • Organisations aiming to reduce waste.

When single-use makes sense

  • Settings without sterilization facilities, such as some community or field settings.
  • Low-volume procedures where reprocessing is not cost-effective.
  • Specific infection control situations defined by local policy.

How to compare

  • Estimate how many times a reusable instrument will be used over its life.
  • Include reprocessing costs: labour, water, energy, consumables.
  • Include waste disposal costs for single-use items.
  • Test both options for performance with your clinicians.

Frequently asked questions

Are single-use instruments safer?

They remove the risk of reprocessing failures, but properly reprocessed reusable instruments are safe and widely used. Follow local infection control policy.

Are reusable instruments more sustainable?

They usually generate far less waste, although reprocessing uses water and energy.

Can a facility use both?

Yes. Many use reusable instruments for most sets and single-use items for specific situations.
