Upper molar forceps are designed for maxillary molars, which usually have three roots: two on the cheek (buccal) side and one on the palate side. Because the root shape is different on each side, most upper molar forceps are made in left and right versions.

In short

Upper molar forceps have one pointed beak that fits between the two buccal roots and one rounded beak for the palatal root. Left and right versions are needed. Third molars have their own patterns.

Why left and right?

On upper molars, the pointed beak must sit on the buccal (cheek) side, where it engages the furcation between the two buccal roots. The buccal side is on the left for left molars and on the right for right molars, so the forceps are made as mirror-image pairs.

Bayonet shapes

Forceps for teeth further back in the upper jaw are often bayonet-shaped, with an offset between handles and beaks. This lets the clinician reach posterior teeth while keeping the handles clear of the lower lip.

Frequently asked questions

Can I use one upper molar forceps for both sides?

Most upper molar patterns are side-specific. Some universal patterns, and third molar forceps such as No. 210, can be used on both sides.

What is the difference between No. 53 and No. 88?

Both are upper molar forceps. No. 88 has longer, more pointed beaks that reach further into the furcation.

How can I tell left from right?

Hold the forceps as in use: the pointed beak should be on the cheek side of the tooth. Most forceps are also marked L or R.
