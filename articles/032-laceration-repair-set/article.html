A laceration repair set is one of the most used instrument sets in emergency departments, urgent care centres and clinics. Because it is opened so often, it should be small, consistent and easy to reprocess. This guide lists the instruments most sets are built from.

In short

A basic laceration repair set needs a needle holder, toothed forceps, suture scissors and one or two hemostats. Add iris scissors for trimming and skin hooks for fine work if your team uses them.

Choosing the needle holder

Most lacerations are closed with fine to medium sutures, so a small needle holder with fine jaws suits most cases. Facial lacerations often use finer sutures, where a Webster or fine-jaw tungsten carbide needle holder works well. Heavier sutures for scalp or limbs may need a slightly larger needle holder.

Variations by setting

  • Facial repair sets add finer forceps, iris scissors and skin hooks.
  • Paediatric sets use the same instruments; compact sizes are often preferred.
  • Nail bed or hand sets may add fine scissors and small elevators.

Tips for reusable sets

Set design checklist

  • Keep the set small; extra instruments add cleaning time.
  • Use the same layout and tray list every time.
  • Choose sturdy instruments; laceration sets see heavy use.
  • Keep spare needle holders and scissors, which wear fastest.

Frequently asked questions

Should a laceration repair set include a scalpel handle?

Some do, for debriding or extending a wound. If your clinicians rarely need one, it can be added separately when needed.

Reusable or disposable laceration sets?

Reusable stainless steel sets usually cost less per use and perform better, if your facility can reprocess them. Disposable sets suit settings without sterilization services.

How many sets does a department need?

Enough to cover the busiest period plus the time it takes to reprocess a set. Track use for a few weeks to estimate it.
