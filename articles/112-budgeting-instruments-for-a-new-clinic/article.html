Setting up a new clinic involves many costs, and instruments are easy to get wrong in both directions: buying too many unused items, or too few sets to run busy sessions. A structured approach to budgeting instruments helps you spend where it matters.

In short

Start from the procedures you will offer, build set lists, calculate how many sets you need including reprocessing time, choose quality where instruments see heavy use, and budget for spares and replacements.

Step 1: Start from procedures

List the procedures the clinic will offer, such as minor surgery, laceration repair, dressings, suture removal or dental treatments. For each, agree a set list with your clinicians.

Step 2: Calculate set quantities

QuestionWhy it matters
How many of each procedure in the busiest session?Sets in use at peak
How long does reprocessing take?Sets unavailable while being cleaned and sterilized
What reserve is needed?Cover for damage, repairs and unexpected demand

Step 3: Choose quality wisely

Spend more on high-use, precision instruments such as scissors, needle holders and dental scalers, where quality affects performance and lifespan. Simple items such as kidney dishes and scalpel handles can be standard quality.

Step 4: Budgeting instruments for their whole life

Budget checklist

  • Instrument sets for each procedure.
  • Spares of high-wear instruments.
  • Trays, cassettes, containers and storage.
  • Reprocessing equipment and consumables.
  • Annual sharpening, repair and replacement.
  • Shipping, duties and taxes on imported instruments.

Step 5: Phase purchases

If budget is tight, buy core sets first and add specialist instruments as services grow. Track usage in the first months to refine quantities before larger orders.

Frequently asked questions

What share of a clinic budget goes on instruments?

It varies widely with services offered. Build the budget from your own procedure list rather than a rule of thumb.

Is it cheaper to buy complete sets?

Sets are convenient, but building to your own lists avoids paying for unused items.

How can I reduce instrument costs?

Standardise sets, buy durable instruments for high-use items, look after them well and repair rather than replace when possible.
