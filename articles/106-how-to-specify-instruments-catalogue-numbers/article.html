Many instrument ordering problems start with a vague description. "Forceps, 14 cm" could mean dozens of different instruments. To specify instruments correctly, include the pattern, size, jaw and tip details, and a reference such as a catalogue number or photo. This guide explains how.

In short

For each instrument, give the pattern name, overall length, straight or curved, jaw or tip type, any inserts or finish, quantity, and a catalogue number or photo of the item you use now.

Catalogue numbers

Every manufacturer uses its own catalogue numbers, so a number from one catalogue will not mean anything to another supplier. Still, sending the number together with the source catalogue, or a photo, helps the new supplier match the item exactly. If you run your own range, create a consistent numbering system and use it on tray lists and orders.

Common mix-ups

  • Kelly and Crile hemostats, which look alike.
  • Mayo and Metzenbaum scissors of similar length.
  • Adson forceps with and without teeth.
  • American and English pattern dental forceps with the same number.
  • Lengths quoted in inches by one supplier and centimetres by another.

Ordering checklist

  • One line per instrument, with all details filled in.
  • Photos for any item that is unusual or hard to describe.
  • Units stated clearly (cm or inches).
  • Samples requested for new or critical items.

Frequently asked questions

Are catalogue numbers universal?

No. Each manufacturer has its own. Pattern names and specifications are what make an order clear.

Should lengths be in centimetres or inches?

Either is fine, but state which. Many catalogues list both.

What if I don't know the pattern name?

Send clear photos of the instrument, including the jaws and any markings.
