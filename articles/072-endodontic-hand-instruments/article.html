Root canal treatment relies on rotary and hand files, but it also needs a group of steel endodontic hand instruments to find canals, remove material, measure and fill. This guide covers the hand instruments found in most endodontic cassettes.

In short

An endodontic cassette usually includes a DG16 explorer, an endodontic excavator, spreaders and pluggers in several sizes, locking pliers and a measuring ruler.

Spreaders and pluggers

Spreaders have pointed tips and are used to make space beside the main cone during lateral condensation. Pluggers have flat ends and push material down the canal. Both are made in graded sizes, often colour-coded, and in hand-held or finger-held versions. Nickel-titanium versions are flexible for curved canals; stainless steel versions are stiffer.

The DG16 explorer

The DG16 is a double-ended explorer with long, fine tips at different angles. Its stiffness helps the clinician feel canal openings on the floor of the pulp chamber.

Buying checklist

  • A range of spreader and plugger sizes that match your obturation technique.
  • Clear size markings or colour codes.
  • Fine, accurate tips on explorers and spreaders.
  • Locking pliers with grooved tips that hold points securely.

Frequently asked questions

What is a DG16 used for?

Finding the openings of root canals on the floor of the pulp chamber.

What is the difference between a spreader and a plugger?

Spreaders are pointed and compact material sideways; pluggers have flat ends and compact material downwards.

Are endodontic hand instruments reusable?

Steel instruments such as explorers, excavators, pluggers and pliers are reusable after cleaning and sterilization. Many files are single-use.
