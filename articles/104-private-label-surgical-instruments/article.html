Many medical distributors sell surgical instruments under their own brand. The instruments are manufactured by a specialist maker, then marked and packed with the distributor's name. Private label surgical instruments can build brand loyalty and margins, but they also bring responsibilities, especially around regulation.

In short

Define the range, approve samples, agree marking and packaging, and be clear about regulatory responsibilities in your markets before you launch.

Planning the range

Start with instruments your customers buy most often, such as scissors, forceps, needle holders and hemostats, and add specialist items later. Use a clear catalogue numbering system from the start, because it will appear on instruments, packaging and price lists.

Marking and packaging

ElementOptions

Catalogue numberYour own numbering, marked on instruments and packs
PackagingIndividual pouches, boxes, labels with your branding
DocumentationInstructions for use, care leaflets, labels in required languages

Regulatory responsibilities

Selling medical devices under your own name can make you the legal manufacturer or carry other obligations in some markets, for example under the EU Medical Device Regulation or FDA rules in the United States. Requirements can include registration, technical documentation, labelling and UDI. Take regulatory advice for each market before launching a private label range, and agree with your manufacturer which documents and support they will provide.

Launch checklist

  • A product list with specifications and catalogue numbers.
  • Approved samples with your marking and packaging.
  • Agreed quality standards and inspection.
  • Regulatory plan for each market.
  • Agreed minimum quantities, lead times and reorder process.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between OEM and private label?

The terms overlap. Private label usually means a manufacturer's existing products sold under your brand; OEM can also include products made to your own designs.

Can small distributors start a private label range?

Yes. Start with a focused range and agree realistic quantities with your manufacturer.

Who is responsible for regulatory compliance?

It depends on the market and how the products are labelled. Take professional regulatory advice.
