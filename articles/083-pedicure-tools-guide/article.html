Pedicures need a different set of tools from manicures. Toenails are thicker, and callus on the feet must be reduced safely. Professional pedicure tools are stronger, larger and, in most salons, made of stainless steel so they can be cleaned and disinfected between clients.

In short

A pedicure kit needs toenail nippers or clippers, a cuticle pusher, cuticle nippers, a nail cleaner or curette, and a foot file or rasp for callus. Stainless steel tools can be disinfected; pumice and emery files are usually single-use.

Foot files and rasps

Stainless steel foot files have a textured or perforated surface that gently reduces hard skin. Some have replaceable abrasive pads on a steel base. Metal rasps with very sharp, grater-like surfaces should be used with care, as they can remove too much skin.

Callus shavers with blades are restricted or banned for salon use in some places; check your local regulations.

Hygiene

Feet can carry fungal and other infections, so hygiene is especially important. Clean and disinfect all reusable pedicure tools between clients, and use single-use items such as pumice and emery files only once.

Kit checklist

  • Strong, sharp toenail nippers with straight jaws.
  • Stainless steel foot files that can be disinfected.
  • Enough sets for back-to-back appointments.
  • Separate storage for clean and used tools.

Frequently asked questions

Are metal foot files better than pumice?

Stainless steel foot files can be disinfected and reused, while pumice is porous and usually single-use.

Can salons use callus razors?

Rules differ by location; in some places blade callus removers are restricted for non-medical practitioners.

How should toenails be cut in a pedicure?

Generally straight across, then smoothed with a file, to reduce the risk of ingrown nails.
