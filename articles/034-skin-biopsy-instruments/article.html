Skin biopsies are among the most frequent procedures in dermatology and general practice. The technique (punch, shave or excision) determines which skin biopsy instruments are needed. Having a well-designed set for each technique makes procedures quicker and more consistent.

In short

Punch biopsies need a punch, fine forceps and fine scissors. Shave biopsies need a blade and fine forceps. Excision biopsies need a full minor set: scalpel, fine forceps, skin hooks, iris scissors and a needle holder.

Punch biopsy

A biopsy punch has a circular blade that cuts a cylinder of skin, commonly 2 to 8 mm across. After the punch is twisted into the skin, the sample is lifted gently with fine forceps or a skin hook (avoiding crushing it) and freed at the base with fine scissors. Most punches are single-use, while the supporting instruments are reusable.

Shave biopsy

A shave biopsy removes a thin layer of skin with a blade held parallel to the surface. A No. 15 scalpel blade or a flexible blade is used, with fine forceps to steady the tissue.

Excision biopsy

An excision biopsy removes the whole lesion with a margin, usually as an ellipse, and the wound is sutured. It needs a basic minor surgery set.

Choosing skin biopsy instruments

Selection checklist

  • Fine-tipped forceps that hold small samples without crushing them.
  • Sharp, fine iris scissors, curved for freeing punch samples.
  • A small needle holder suited to fine sutures.
  • Skin hooks for lifting samples and wound edges gently.

Frequently asked questions

Why avoid gripping the sample with toothed forceps?

Crushing the tissue can distort it and make it harder for the laboratory to examine. Lifting it gently with a skin hook or fine forceps at the edge is better.

Are biopsy punches reusable?

Most biopsy punches used today are single-use. The forceps, scissors, hooks and needle holders in the set are reusable.

Can one set cover all three techniques?

A well-equipped excision set can handle punch and shave biopsies too, but separate small sets are quicker to reprocess.
