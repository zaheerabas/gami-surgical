Thick toenails are hard to cut with ordinary clippers, which can crush or split the nail. Toenail nippers are designed for the job, with long handles for leverage and strong, sharp jaws that cut through thick nails cleanly. They are used by podiatrists, pedicurists and carers, and many people buy them for home use.

In short

Choose toenail nippers with long handles, strong jaws, a reliable spring and a smooth joint. Straight jaws cut across the nail; concave jaws follow its curve. Cut in small bites rather than one large cut.

What makes toenail nippers different

  • Long handles multiply hand force, so less effort is needed.
  • Strong jaws cut through thick nail without bending.
  • Sharp, accurately ground edges cut cleanly rather than crushing.
  • Springs (double leaf or barrel) open the jaws after each cut.

Jaw shapes

JawUse

Cutting thick nails safely

Softening the nails first, for example after a warm foot soak, makes cutting easier. Cut in small bites, working across the nail, rather than trying to cut the whole width at once. People with diabetes, poor circulation or very thick, painful nails should see a podiatrist rather than cutting their own nails.

Quality checklist

  • The cutting edges meet evenly to the tips.
  • The joint is firm with no side-to-side movement.
  • The spring opens the jaws fully and evenly.
  • The handles are comfortable and give a secure grip.
  • Stainless steel that can be cleaned and sterilized.

Frequently asked questions

Are toenail nippers better than clippers for thick nails?

Yes, generally. Nippers have more leverage and stronger jaws, so they cut thick nails more cleanly.

Which spring type is best?

Double leaf springs and barrel springs both work well; choose by feel and durability.

How do I keep toenail nippers sharp?

Use them only on nails, clean and dry them after use, oil the joint occasionally, and have them sharpened professionally.
