Instruments wear with use: scissors dull, jaws misalign, ratchets loosen and inserts wear. Many of these problems can be fixed, extending the instrument's life for a fraction of the price of a new one. But some damage means the instrument should be retired. A simple approach to instrument repair saves money and keeps unsafe instruments out of use.

In short

Repair instruments that are dull, misaligned or stiff. Replace instruments that are cracked, pitted, heavily corroded, or have been repaired so often that they no longer perform reliably.

What repair services can usually fix

Dull scissors, curettes, osteotomesSharpening

Worn tungsten carbide insertsInsert replacement
Surface stainingRefinishing and passivation

When to replace

  • Cracks, especially in box locks, jaws or ring handles.
  • Pitting that cannot be removed without weakening the instrument.
  • Bends or breaks in fine tips that cannot be restored.
  • Instruments that have been sharpened so often that the blades are thin or misshapen.
  • Repair costs that approach the price of a new instrument.

Cost per use

Comparing repair and replacement is easier when you think in cost per use. A quality instrument that can be sharpened and adjusted several times over its life often costs less per use than cheaper instruments replaced frequently.

Running an instrument repair programme

Programme checklist

  • Inspect instruments at every reprocessing cycle and tag those needing repair.
  • Schedule regular sharpening for high-use cutting instruments.
  • Keep spare instruments so sets stay complete while items are away.
  • Record repairs to spot instruments that fail repeatedly.
  • Use repair providers who restore original angles and finishes.

Frequently asked questions

How many times can scissors be sharpened?

It depends on the design and how much metal is removed each time. Good scissors can often be sharpened several times.

Can corroded instruments be saved?

Light surface staining can often be removed. Pitting usually means the instrument should be replaced.

Should instruments be repaired by the manufacturer?

Not necessarily; many independent repair services are skilled. Choose one that restores original specifications.
