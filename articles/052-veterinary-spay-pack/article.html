Spays (ovariohysterectomies) are among the most frequent procedures in small-animal practice, so most clinics keep dedicated veterinary spay packs. A well-designed veterinary spay pack contains everything needed for the procedure, laid out the same way every time.

Note

Instrument preferences vary between vets and techniques. Use this list as a starting point and adjust it with your surgical team.

Key instruments explained

The spay hook has a smooth, blunt hook that is passed along the abdominal wall to lift the uterine horn through a small incision. Rochester-Carmalt forceps have longitudinal jaw serrations with cross-hatching at the tips, which help hold vascular pedicles securely. The Olsen-Hegar needle holder lets a vet working alone tie and cut sutures without changing instruments.

Keeping spay packs consistent

Pack checklist

  • Use a written pack list and the same layout for every pack.
  • Count instruments before and after each procedure.
  • Keep spare spay hooks and Carmalt forceps; they are easily lost or worn.
  • Inspect needle holder jaws and scissors regularly.

Frequently asked questions

Are Carmalt forceps different from Kelly forceps?

Yes. Rochester-Carmalt forceps have longitudinal serrations designed to hold pedicles securely, while Kelly forceps have transverse serrations.

Which spay hook is better, Snook or Covault?

Both work well; the choice is mostly a matter of preference. Covault hooks are slightly larger.

How many spay packs does a clinic need?

Enough for a busy surgery day plus the time needed to reprocess packs.
