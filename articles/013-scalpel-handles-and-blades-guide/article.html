Scalpel handles and blades are the most basic cutting instruments in surgery, but ordering them still causes confusion. Blades are numbered, handles are numbered, and not every blade fits every handle. This guide explains the common sizes, what each blade shape is for, and how to handle them safely.

In short

No. 3 handles take the small blades (such as 10, 11, 12 and 15). No. 4 handles take the large blades (20 to 25). Choose the blade shape for the cut, and always fit and remove blades with an instrument, never with fingers.

Reusable handles, single-use blades

In most settings the handle is a reusable stainless steel instrument, while the blades are sterile and single-use. The handle has a slotted fitting onto which the blade slides and locks. Handles come in a few standard fittings, and each fitting accepts a family of blades.

Handles are also made with graduated rulers etched on the side, with textured grips, or in lightweight designs. Check the fitting, not just the shape, when ordering.

Common blade shapes

  • No. 10: a curved cutting edge (a "belly") for longer skin incisions.
  • No. 11: a straight, pointed triangular blade for stab incisions, such as draining an abscess or making a small opening.
  • No. 12: a small, hooked blade sharpened on the inner curve, used for example to cut sutures or in some ENT and dental work.
  • No. 15: a small curved blade for short, precise incisions, and probably the most used blade in minor surgery.
  • No. 20 to 25: larger versions with curved edges for large incisions, used on No. 4 handles.

Fitting scalpel handles and blades safely

Most sharps injuries with scalpels happen when blades are fitted or removed. Hold the blade with a needle holder or a dedicated blade remover, never with fingers, and point the cutting edge away from yourself and others. Many facilities use single-handed blade removers or safety scalpels to reduce risk further.

Safe handling checklist

  • Fit the blade with a needle holder, sliding it onto the fitting until it clicks.
  • Pass scalpels in a neutral zone or tray rather than hand to hand.
  • Remove used blades with an instrument or blade remover.
  • Dispose of blades straight into a sharps container.

Looking after scalpel handles

Handles are simple but still need care. Clean the fitting carefully, as tissue can collect in the slot, and inspect it for wear. A worn fitting lets the blade wobble, which reduces control and is a safety risk. Replace handles whose fitting no longer holds blades firmly.

Frequently asked questions

Does a No. 15 blade fit a No. 4 handle?

No. The No. 15 blade belongs to the small blade family that fits No. 3, No. 3L and No. 7 handles. No. 4 handles take the larger blades from No. 20 to No. 25.

Can scalpel blades be resterilized?

Standard scalpel blades are sold sterile for single use. Reusing them dulls the edge and adds infection risk, so they should go into a sharps container after use. The handle is the reusable part.

Which handle should a minor surgery set include?

Most minor surgery sets include a No. 3 handle, which takes the No. 10, 11 and 15 blades used for most small procedures. A No. 7 handle can be added for fine work.
