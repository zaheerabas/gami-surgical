Dental elevators are used to loosen teeth and roots before or instead of using extraction forceps. They work by controlled leverage, wedging and rotation. A small selection of well-chosen dental elevators covers most extractions in general practice.

In short

Straight and Coupland elevators are general-purpose. Warwick James elevators are small and precise. Cryer and Winter elevators come in left and right pairs for roots. Apical elevators retrieve root tips.

Left and right pairs

Many dental elevators, such as Cryer, Winter and curved Warwick James, are made in left and right versions so the blade can be applied from either side. Order them in pairs unless you know only one side is needed.

Handles

Elevators have straight handles, pear-shaped handles that fit the palm, or cross-bar handles. Cross-bar handles give more leverage but need careful control. A comfortable, secure grip helps the clinician apply force safely.

Safe use

Elevators can generate considerable force. The clinician's other hand normally supports the jaw and protects soft tissue, and adjacent teeth should not be used as a fulcrum unless they are also being extracted. Clinical technique belongs to clinical training; this guide covers the instruments only.

Quality checklist

  • Blade tips are sharp, straight and free of chips.
  • The blade is firmly attached to the handle.
  • Left and right versions are clearly marked.
  • The handle gives a secure, comfortable grip.

Frequently asked questions

What is a Coupland elevator?

A straight elevator with a gouge-shaped blade, made in graded sizes, used to wedge between tooth and bone and loosen the tooth.

Why do Cryer elevators come in pairs?

The triangular blade is angled, so left and right versions are needed to work from either side.

Can elevator tips be sharpened?

Yes, a repair service can resharpen elevators, but badly worn or bent tips should be replaced.
