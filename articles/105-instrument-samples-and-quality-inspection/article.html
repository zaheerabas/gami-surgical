The safest way to buy instruments from a new supplier, or to launch a new product, is to test samples first and then check that the order matches them. Samples and quality inspection protect buyers from surprises and give manufacturers a clear reference.

In short

Test samples carefully, keep approved samples as the reference ("golden samples"), agree inspection criteria in writing, and inspect orders before or on arrival using a sampling plan.

Testing samples

Sample test checklist

  • Dimensions and pattern match your specification.
  • Jaws and tips align; teeth and serrations mesh.
  • Scissors cut cleanly to the tips; needle holders grip needles.
  • Ratchets, joints and springs work smoothly.
  • Finish is smooth, with no burrs, pits or marks.
  • Markings and packaging are correct.
  • Instruments survive several cleaning and sterilization cycles without staining.

Golden samples

Once a sample is approved, keep it (and ideally a matching one at the manufacturer) as the reference for production. Any change in design, finish or packaging should be agreed in writing and a new sample approved.

Inspecting orders: AQL sampling

For larger orders, it is not practical to test every instrument. Buyers often use acceptance sampling based on standards such as ISO 2859-1, choosing an acceptable quality limit (AQL) for different defect categories. A random sample is inspected, and the batch is accepted or rejected depending on the number of defects found.

Defect categoryExamplesTypical approach
CriticalCracks, broken parts, safety issuesZero tolerance
MajorMisaligned jaws, dull scissors, failing ratchetsStrict AQL
MinorSmall cosmetic marks, packaging issuesMore lenient AQL

Who carries out quality inspection?

The manufacturer should inspect every batch. Buyers can add their own checks on arrival, or use an independent inspection company before shipment for large orders.

Frequently asked questions

Do suppliers charge for samples?

Policies vary. Some charge for samples or shipping and credit the cost against the first order.

What is a golden sample?

An approved sample kept as the reference that production must match.

What AQL should I use?

It depends on the product and risk. Many buyers use zero tolerance for critical defects and set stricter limits for major than minor defects.
