Podiatrists and chiropodists treat nails, corns, calluses and other foot conditions with a compact kit of hand instruments. Because the same instruments are used on every patient, quality, comfort and reliable reprocessing are essential. This guide lists the core podiatry instruments.

In short

A basic podiatry kit includes heavy and fine nail nippers, an ingrown nail tool, a scalpel handle, nail files or rasps, a probe and an excavator. Most practices keep several identical kits so each can be sterilized between patients.

The core podiatry instruments

InstrumentPurpose
Nail nippers (straight or concave jaws)Cutting thick or thin nails
Ingrown or fine-pointed nippersReaching nail edges and corners
Scalpel handle (often No. 3)Debriding corns and callus with blades such as No. 10 and 15
Nail files and raspsSmoothing and reducing nails
Black's file or nail elevatorClearing the nail sulcus
ProbeExploring the nail sulcus
Excavator or curetteRemoving debris and enucleating corns

Nail nippers for podiatry

Podiatry nippers have stronger jaws than manicure nippers, with long handles for leverage on thick nails. Straight jaws cut nails straight across; concave jaws follow the nail's curve. Fine, pointed nippers reach the edges of ingrown nails. Double-spring and barrel-spring designs are both common.

How many kits?

Each patient needs a sterile kit, and reprocessing takes time. Practices typically keep enough kits for a full clinic session plus a reserve. Count the number of patients per session and the time taken to clean and sterilize kits to work out the number you need.

Care checklist

  • Clean instruments promptly; nail debris dries hard on jaws and joints.
  • Open nippers fully when cleaning and sterilizing.
  • Lubricate joints regularly to keep the action smooth.
  • Have nippers sharpened when they start to crush rather than cut.

Frequently asked questions

Are podiatry nippers different from salon nail nippers?

Podiatry nippers are usually heavier, with stronger jaws and longer handles for thick nails, and are made to withstand sterilization between patients.

Which scalpel handle do podiatrists use?

Many use a No. 3 handle with No. 10 or 15 blades, though preferences vary.

Can podiatry instruments be autoclaved?

Stainless steel podiatry instruments can be cleaned and steam sterilized, and in many places this is required between patients.
