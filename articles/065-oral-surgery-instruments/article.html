Surgical extractions, wisdom tooth removal and minor oral surgery need more than forceps and elevators. Oral surgery instruments are used to raise gum flaps, protect soft tissue, smooth bone and close the wound. This guide covers the instruments most oral surgery kits include.

In short

A minor oral surgery kit typically adds a scalpel handle, periosteal elevator, cheek and tongue retractors, bone file, rongeur, curette, needle holder, scissors and surgical suction to the extraction instruments.

Periosteal elevators

Periosteal elevators lift the gum and periosteum away from the bone to create a flap. The Molt No. 9 is a widely used double-ended pattern with a pointed end and a broader rounded end. Sharp, well-shaped ends help raise flaps cleanly.

Building an oral surgery kit

Kit checklist

  • Standardise the kit for the procedures you do most often.
  • Keep extraction forceps and elevators in a separate cassette.
  • Include at least one sturdy retractor for the cheek and one for the flap.
  • Check that needle holders suit the suture sizes you use.

Frequently asked questions

What is a Molt periosteal elevator?

A double-ended periosteal elevator (commonly No. 9) used to raise gum flaps from the bone.

Why use a bone file?

To smooth sharp bone edges after an extraction so the gum heals comfortably.

Are dental rongeurs different from orthopaedic rongeurs?

They are smaller and lighter, designed for the jaw, but work in the same way.
