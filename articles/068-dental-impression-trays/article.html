Dental impression trays carry impression material into the mouth and hold it while it sets. A tray that fits well and holds the material securely gives more accurate impressions. Reusable stainless steel trays remain popular because they are rigid, durable and can be sterilized many times.

In short

Choose rigid trays that fit the arch, with perforations or a rim-lock edge to hold the material. Keep a full range of upper and lower sizes, plus partial trays if you take sectional impressions.

Why rigidity matters

If a tray flexes while the impression is removed, the set material can distort. Stainless steel trays are stiff and keep their shape, which helps produce accurate impressions. Disposable plastic trays are convenient but vary in rigidity.

How trays hold the material

DesignHow it retains material
PerforatedMaterial flows through holes and locks in place
Rim-lockA bead or rim around the edge holds the material, often used with alginate
Solid (non-perforated)Relies on tray adhesive

Tray adhesive is often used with all designs to improve retention.

Sizes and shapes

Impression trays are made for upper (maxillary) and lower (mandibular) arches, in several sizes from small to extra large, and for dentate and edentulous patients. Partial or quadrant trays cover part of the arch. Having a full size range lets the clinician choose a tray that leaves a few millimetres of space for the material all round.

Buying checklist

  • A complete range of upper and lower sizes.
  • Perforated or rim-lock design to suit your impression materials.
  • Smooth, rounded edges that are comfortable for patients.
  • Sturdy handles that do not bend.
  • Size markings stamped on each tray.

Cleaning reusable trays

Remove all set impression material and adhesive before cleaning, using a suitable adhesive remover if needed. Then clean, inspect and sterilize the trays like other dental instruments.

Frequently asked questions

Are stainless steel impression trays better than plastic?

Steel trays are more rigid and reusable. Plastic trays are convenient and single-use. Many clinics keep both.

What is a rim-lock tray?

A tray with a raised bead around the rim that helps hold the impression material, commonly used with alginate.

How many trays does a clinic need?

Enough of each size to cover a day's impressions plus reprocessing time.
