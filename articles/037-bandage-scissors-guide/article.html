Bandage scissors are designed to cut and remove dressings, bandages and tape close to the skin without injuring the patient. Nurses, paramedics and clinic staff use them many times a day, so a comfortable, sharp, durable pair makes a real difference.

In short

Lister bandage scissors have an angled shape and a broad, blunt tip on the lower blade that slides under dressings safely. Choose a size that suits your hand and the dressings you remove, and keep them for dressings only.

The Lister pattern

Lister scissors are angled at the joint, so the hand stays above the patient while the blades run flat along the skin. The lower blade ends in a flattened, rounded tip (sometimes called a probe or knob tip) that slides under a bandage without catching or cutting the skin.

Sizes

Lister bandage scissors are commonly made from around 14 cm to 20 cm. Smaller pairs fit easily in a pocket; larger pairs cut thick bandages and plaster padding more easily.

Choosing good bandage scissors

Quality checklist

  • The blunt tip is smooth and rounded, with no sharp edges.
  • The blades cut cleanly through layers of bandage, right to the tip.
  • The action is smooth and the screw does not loosen.
  • Stainless steel that can be cleaned and disinfected between patients.

Frequently asked questions

Why is the tip of bandage scissors blunt?

The blunt, flattened tip slides under the dressing without catching or cutting the skin.

Can bandage scissors be sterilized?

Stainless steel bandage scissors can be cleaned, disinfected and steam sterilized. Scissors with plastic handles may have temperature limits; check the manufacturer's instructions.

Are bandage scissors the same as trauma shears?

Trauma shears (tough-cut scissors) are heavier and designed for clothing and thick materials. Lister scissors are finer and better for dressings close to the skin.
