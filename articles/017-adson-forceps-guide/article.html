Adson forceps are among the most used thumb forceps in minor surgery, dermatology and skin closure. Their broad handle gives a comfortable grip, and the fine tips allow precise handling of skin edges. Several versions exist, and choosing the right one makes suturing easier.

In short

Choose toothed Adson forceps for skin edges, non-toothed for dressings and sutures, and Brown-Adson when you want a gentle, many-toothed grip on delicate skin.

What makes an Adson forceps

The Adson pattern is short, usually around 12 cm, with a wide, flat handle that narrows sharply to fine tips. The wide handle gives control without pinching hard, and the fine tips let the user pick up small amounts of tissue precisely.

Why toothed forceps are kinder to skin

It may seem that teeth would damage skin more than smooth tips, but the opposite is often true. A small toothed grip holds the skin edge securely with light pressure, while smooth tips need to be squeezed harder, which can crush the tissue. For skin closure, many clinicians therefore prefer toothed or Brown-Adson forceps.

Tips: fine, standard or angled

Adson forceps are made with fine and standard tip widths, and in straight or angled versions. Fine tips suit facial and plastic work; standard tips are more robust for general use. Angled tips can improve visibility in some areas.

Quality points

Before you buy

  • Close the forceps: the tips should meet exactly, with teeth meshing fully.
  • The spring should feel even and not too stiff.
  • The guide pin, if present, should align the tips smoothly.
  • The handle serrations should give a secure grip with gloves.

Care

Fine tips are easily bent, so protect them with tip guards during cleaning and sterilization, and avoid dropping instruments into trays. Check alignment regularly and send forceps for repair if the tips cross or gap.

Frequently asked questions

How long are Adson forceps?

Standard Adson forceps are about 12 cm long. Longer and shorter variations exist, so state the length when ordering.

What is the difference between Adson and Brown-Adson forceps?

Adson forceps have a single 1x2 tooth arrangement or plain serrations. Brown-Adson forceps have rows of small interlocking teeth along each tip for a gentle, secure grip on delicate skin.

Are tungsten carbide tips worth it?

For forceps used every day, tungsten carbide tips keep their grip much longer than plain steel, which reduces repairs and replacements.
