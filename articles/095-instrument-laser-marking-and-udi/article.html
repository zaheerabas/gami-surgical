Every quality instrument carries markings: the manufacturer or brand, a catalogue number, and often a size or pattern. Instrument laser marking has become the standard method because it is permanent, precise and does not create crevices where debris can collect. Markings also support traceability, which regulators increasingly require.

In short

Laser marking gives permanent, smooth markings for logos, catalogue numbers and machine-readable codes. In some markets, unique device identification (UDI) rules require identifiers on medical devices, including direct marking on certain reusable instruments.

Why laser marking?

Older methods such as stamping or acid etching can leave rough grooves where debris and moisture collect. Laser marking changes the colour of the surface layer with very little material removal, giving clear, smooth marks. Correct laser settings matter: overly deep or rough marking can harm corrosion resistance, so marked instruments should still be finished and passivated properly.

UDI in brief

Regulations in markets such as the United States and the European Union require many medical devices to carry a unique device identifier, made up of a device identifier and production information. For certain reusable devices that are reprocessed, the rules call for the identifier to be marked directly on the device. Requirements depend on the device class and market, so manufacturers and importers should check the rules that apply to their products.

Instrument tracking in hospitals

Many sterile services departments use data matrix codes on instruments or trays to record which instruments were used, cleaned and sterilized for each set. This improves inventory control and traceability.

Questions to ask suppliers

  • Is marking done by laser, and is it smooth to the touch?
  • Can you add our logo or catalogue numbers?
  • Can you apply data matrix codes for tracking systems?
  • How do you handle UDI for the markets we sell in?

Frequently asked questions

Does laser marking wear off?

Good laser marking is very durable, though aggressive cleaning and polishing over many years can fade it.

Can a distributor's brand be marked instead of the manufacturer's?

Often yes, as private label marking, but regulatory responsibilities must be clear. Discuss this with your manufacturer and regulator.

Can existing instruments be laser marked?

Many repair and tracking providers can add marks to existing instruments.
