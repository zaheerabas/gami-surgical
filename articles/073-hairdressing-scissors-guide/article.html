For hairdressers and barbers, scissors are the most important tool they own. Professional hairdressing scissors should cut cleanly without pushing or folding the hair, feel balanced in the hand, and stay sharp through many cuts. This guide explains the features that separate a professional pair from a cheap one.

In short

Choose a handle shape that keeps your hand relaxed, an edge that suits your cutting style, a blade length for the techniques you use, and good steel with an adjustable tension screw.

Handle designs

HandleDescriptionBenefit
Classic (opposing)Symmetrical ringsTraditional, suits some techniques
OffsetThumb ring shorter than the finger ringMore natural hand position, less strain
CraneOffset with an angled finger handleLower elbow and shoulder, more ergonomic
Swivel thumbRotating thumb ringReduces wrist and thumb strain

Most hairdressing scissors also have a finger rest (tang) for the little finger, which improves control.

Edges: convex or bevelled

  • Convex edges are very sharp and smooth, suited to slide cutting and precision work.
  • Bevelled edges are more durable and often have fine serrations that grip the hair, suited to blunt cutting and beginners.

Blade length

Hairdressing scissors are measured in inches, commonly from about 5 to 7 inches. Shorter scissors give precision for detail work; longer scissors suit scissor-over-comb and barbering techniques.

Steel and tension

Good steel holds a sharp edge longer and resists corrosion. An adjustable tension screw lets the stylist set the blades so they close smoothly without being loose. Correct tension is checked by lifting the finger blade and letting it fall: it should close partway, not snap shut or stay open.

Care checklist

  • Wipe hair and moisture off after each client.
  • Oil the pivot area daily.
  • Check and adjust tension regularly.
  • Store in a case; never drop them.
  • Have them professionally sharpened by a specialist in hair scissors.

Frequently asked questions

What size hairdressing scissors should a beginner buy?

Many stylists start with around 5.5 inches, which balances control and versatility.

Are offset handles better?

Many stylists find offset or crane handles more comfortable because they reduce strain on the hand and shoulder.

Can hairdressing scissors cut paper?

They can, but it dulls the edge quickly. Keep them for hair only.
