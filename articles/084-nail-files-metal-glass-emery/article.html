Nail files shape the free edge of the nail and smooth it after cutting. The material and the coarseness (grit) of the file decide how quickly it removes nail and how smooth the edge is. Choosing the right nail files for clients and retail customers is simpler once you know the differences.

In short

Glass files give a very smooth edge and are gentle. Metal files are durable and can be disinfected. Emery boards are cheap and come in many grits but are usually single-use in salons.

What grit means

Grit describes how coarse the abrasive is. Lower numbers are coarser and remove more nail; higher numbers are finer and smooth the edge. Coarse grits (around 80 to 100) are used on artificial nails; medium grits (around 180) are common for natural nails; fine grits (240 and above) are for finishing and buffing.

Filing technique

Filing in one direction, rather than sawing back and forth, helps prevent splitting and peeling on natural nails. Fine files and gentle pressure are kinder to thin or weak nails.

Choosing nail files

  • Choose grit for the nail type: finer for natural nails.
  • Use disinfectable files (metal or glass) as reusable salon tools.
  • Use emery boards once per client in professional settings.
  • For retail, consider durable steel or glass files in protective cases.

Frequently asked questions

Which nail file is gentlest on natural nails?

Glass files and fine-grit files are generally gentlest.

Can metal nail files be disinfected?

Yes. Stainless steel files can be cleaned and disinfected, which makes them suitable as reusable salon tools.

What grit should I use for natural nails?

Around 180 grit is common for shaping; higher grits for finishing.
