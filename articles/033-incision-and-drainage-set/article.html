Incision and drainage (I&D) of abscesses is carried out every day in emergency departments, clinics and general practice. A well-planned incision and drainage set means the clinician has everything needed to open, explore, clean and pack the cavity without waiting for extra instruments.

In short

An incision and drainage set needs a scalpel handle, a hemostat to break up loculations, a probe, a curette, scissors and forceps. Keep it compact and consistent.

A typical incision and drainage set

InstrumentPurpose
Scalpel handle No. 3 (with No. 11 or 15 blade)Making the incision
Curved hemostat (mosquito or Kelly)Opening the cavity and breaking up pockets
Malleable probeExploring the depth and extent of the cavity
CuretteRemoving debris from the cavity wall
Iris or Metzenbaum scissorsTrimming skin edges and cutting packing
Forceps (toothed and non-toothed)Handling tissue and packing
Gallipot and kidney dishIrrigation fluid and waste

Why each instrument matters

A No. 11 blade makes a controlled stab incision, while a No. 15 is often preferred for a short linear cut. After the incision, a curved hemostat is inserted and gently opened to break up loculations (separate pockets of pus). A probe helps judge the depth and direction of the cavity, and a curette clears debris before irrigation and packing.

Practical tips

Set design checklist

  • Include at least one curved hemostat; it is the most used instrument in I&D.
  • Choose a sturdy curette; abscess cavities are hard on fine instruments.
  • Add a second hemostat if packing is common.
  • Keep the set separate from clean minor surgery sets to avoid mixing.

Reprocessing after drainage

Instruments used in abscess drainage are heavily soiled. Keep them moist, clean them promptly with an enzymatic detergent, and inspect hinges and curette cups carefully before sterilization.

Frequently asked questions

Which scalpel blade is best for incision and drainage?

Many clinicians use a No. 11 blade for a stab incision or a No. 15 for a short linear incision. Both fit a No. 3 handle.

Does an I&D set need a needle holder?

Usually not, since abscess incisions are normally left open. Some sets include one in case a bleeding point needs a suture.

How large should the curette be?

A medium cup is versatile. Larger cavities may need a larger curette.
