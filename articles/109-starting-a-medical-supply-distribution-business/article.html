Distributing instruments can be a rewarding business: hospitals, clinics, dentists, vets and salons need a steady supply, and they value reliable partners. Starting a medical supply distribution business takes planning around market, product range, suppliers and regulation. This overview covers the main steps.

Note

This is general information, not legal or regulatory advice. Rules for medical devices differ between countries; take professional advice for your market.

1. Choose your market

Decide which customers you will serve: hospitals, private clinics, dental practices, veterinary clinics, beauty salons, or a mix. Each has different buying processes, product needs and price expectations. Starting with one segment and serving it well is often easier than trying to cover everything.

2. Build a focused range

CustomerStarter range
Clinics and GPsMinor surgery instruments, suture sets, dressing instruments
Dental practicesExamination instruments, scalers and curettes, extraction forceps
VetsGeneral surgical instruments, spay packs
SalonsNippers, pushers, tweezers, scissors, manicure kits

3. Find reliable manufacturers

Look for manufacturers who control quality, provide samples, communicate clearly and can ship reliably to your country. Test samples, start with a modest order and build the relationship. Consider private label once you know what sells.

4. Understand compliance

Surgical and dental instruments are medical devices in many countries. You may need to register as an importer or distributor, keep records, meet labelling requirements and handle complaints and recalls. Beauty tools usually have lighter requirements, but labelling rules still apply.

5. Plan stock and sales

Business checklist

  • Stock fast-moving items; order slow movers on demand.
  • Know your lead times and plan reorders around them.
  • Offer clear catalogues with specifications and photos.
  • Provide after-sales support, such as replacements and repairs.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a licence to sell surgical instruments?

In many countries, yes, or at least registration. Check with your national regulator.

Should I start with my own brand?

Many distributors start with the manufacturer's products and add private label once they know their customers.

How much stock should I hold?

Enough of your best sellers to cover demand during the manufacturer's lead time, plus a safety margin.
