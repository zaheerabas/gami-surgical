Dental explorers are fine, flexible, pointed instruments used to feel tooth surfaces. Clinicians use them to detect calculus, irregular restoration margins and surface defects. The shape of the tip decides which surfaces each explorer reaches best, so most clinics stock more than one design of dental explorers.

In short

The shepherd's hook (No. 23) is the general-purpose examination explorer. Pigtail and cowhorn explorers reach proximal surfaces, and the ODU 11/12 is designed for subgingival calculus detection.

Single- or double-ended

Many explorers are double-ended, combining two tips (for example a shepherd's hook with a No. 17) or a probe and an explorer. Double-ended instruments reduce the number of instruments in the cassette.

Tactile sensitivity

A good explorer transmits small vibrations from the tip to the fingers. Thin, flexible shanks and lightweight or hollow handles improve this tactile feedback. A worn or bent tip reduces sensitivity and can scratch surfaces.

Care checklist

  • Inspect tips regularly under magnification.
  • Keep explorer tips protected in cassettes.
  • Replace explorers with blunt or bent tips.
  • Do not use explorers to remove calculus; use scalers instead.

Frequently asked questions

What is a shepherd's hook explorer?

The No. 23 explorer, named for its curved shape. It is the most common general examination explorer.

Which explorer is best for subgingival calculus?

The ODU 11/12 and No. 17 are commonly used for subgingival exploration.

Can explorers be sharpened?

Explorer tips are very fine and are usually replaced rather than sharpened.
